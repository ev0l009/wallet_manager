## **2. Suggested additions**

These are the actual requirements I'd want reflected in the document.

**A. Transaction reversal**

- A transaction reversal must be linked to its original transaction.
- The original transaction must remain in the transaction history.
- A successfully reversed transaction must no longer contribute to effective balance calculations or historical reports.
- A reversal must not cause an account's balance to become negative.
- If the available balance is insufficient, the reversal must be rejected without modifying the existing transaction or account state.

**B. Reporting semantics**

We're adopting retrospective correction.

For example, if an income transaction recorded in September is reversed in October, September's report must exclude that income.

The reversal date should still be retained for historical traceability.

**C. Architecture and separation of concerns**

Document the following responsibilities:

- Models: domain representation, internal validation and entity invariants.
- Storage: serialization, deserialization and persistence.
- Business layer (`wallet_engine`): business rules, operation coordination and interactions between models and storage.

One important qualification: cross-entity business rules, such as checking whether an account has sufficient funds for a reversal, belong in the business layer.

**D. Transaction ordering**

- Primary ordering: transaction date.
- Secondary ordering: original insertion order for transactions sharing the same date.
- Creation timestamps and explicit ordering identifiers are deferred improvements.

**E. Persistence**

Document that all data-modifying operations use temporary-file writes followed by atomic replacement of the original file.

This includes account creation, account updates, account deactivation and transaction operations.

**F. Explicit scope exclusion**

Keep these outside the current implementation:

- Storage schema versioning.
- Automatic schema migrations.
- Automated backup and recovery mechanisms.

Your existing atomic persistence mechanism remains part of the current scope.

## **3. One thing I would not change**

I wouldn't modify the proposed project structure in the design document yet.

Your implementation may have a simpler structure, and that isn't automatically a problem. We should first understand what responsibilities your existing modules actually perform.

A project with fewer files and clear boundaries is preferable to one with numerous folders that merely create the appearance of good architecture.
